You get two quotes for the same job. One is a few hundred dollars, the other is several times that, and neither says what you actually receive. That is the normal state of the Perth market, because engineers quote each job and almost none publish a fee.

So price alone will not tell you who to hire. This guide covers what to ask a residential structural engineer before you pay, how to check who you are dealing with, and which claims to treat carefully. It applies whether you want a wall out, a crack looked at, an extension designed or a patio certified.

## Why "structural engineer" on a website tells you very little

In Western Australia the words "engineer", "structural engineer" and "consulting engineer" are not reserved titles at the moment. Anyone can put them on a van. What the law does reserve are titles such as "Registered Building Engineer" and the related "Registered Building Engineering Practitioner" and "Contractor" variants under the Building Services (Registration) Act 2011 and its regulations. Using those without being registered carries a fine of up to $25,000.

Registration of structural building engineers began on 1 July 2024. A transition period runs until 1 July 2027. After that date, structural engineering work on buildings has to be done by a registered building engineer, and unregistered work becomes an offence with a fine of up to $25,000. Some older pages still say 1 July 2026. That date has been superseded.

The practical point is simple. Before you pay anyone, ask for their registration class and number, and check it yourself. The Department of Mines, Industry Regulation and Safety (Building and Energy) keeps a Building Engineer Register, and the [WA Government building engineering registration page](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/multi-step-guides/building-engineering-registration) explains how the scheme works. During the transition, a firm that is not yet on the register is not automatically a bad choice, but you should know why, and how they plan to be registered by 2027.

## Which of these are you actually buying?

Many people search for a structural engineer and end up booking someone else. The Perth market mixes several different products under similar names, and the differences matter when a council, a bank or an insurer reads the result.

| What you might be quoted for | Who usually does it | What it is | Published Perth price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-purchase building inspection (AS 4349.1) | Building inspector | Visual condition report on defects | From $440 including GST to about $550 (one firm's structural and pest package starts at $550, GST not stated) |
| Engineer's crack or structural report | Structural engineer | Judgement on cause, movement and what to do | None published; quoted per job |
| Beam design for a wall removal | Structural engineer | Calculations and drawings for a permit | None published; quoted per job |
| Standard (not site-specific) certified drawings | Online drawing supplier | Pre-made detail for a steel patio | A$330 (GST not stated); brick or limestone screen walls A$275 |
| Site-specific patio or shed certificate | Structural engineer | Drawings and certificate for your slab and soil | None published; quoted per job |

The building-inspection prices in the first row are real Perth figures, but they buy a building inspector's report. One Perth firm says outright that it does not provide structural design, engineering drawings or engineering certification. That is fine as a product. It is not the same thing as an engineer's report, and it will not satisfy a building surveyor who needs a technical certificate. Our guide to [structural inspection versus building inspection](/blog/structural-inspection-vs-building-inspection/) sets out the difference in more detail.

The A$330 steel patio drawings are standard details, not a design for your site. Whether a given council accepts a standard detail for your block is not something a price list can tell you.

> **Interstate / national figures, not Perth prices.** A national quote platform reported, across about 370 projects in 2026, $560 for a residential inspection and report (excluding GST), a $150 hourly rate, $1,900 for beam design for a wall removal and $5,000 for extension design. Those are Australia-wide numbers with no Perth breakdown. Use them to sense-check a quote that looks wildly out, not to predict your own.

## Ten questions to ask before you say yes

You do not need engineering knowledge to run this list. You are checking for clear answers, and clear answers are usually a good sign.

1. **Which registration class are you, and what is your number?** Then look it up on the DEMIRS register yourself.
2. **Will an engineer visit, or a building inspector?** A visual inspection by a building inspector and a site visit by an engineer are different jobs.
3. **What is in the quote, line by line?** Site visit, written report, calculations, drawings, certificate, and any calls to the council or building surveyor.
4. **What happens if the council or surveyor asks a question?** Some quotes include one round of responses. Others bill it separately.
5. **Will your certificate match my council's form?** The City of Perth uses a form titled "Certificate of Structural Sufficiency". Other councils word theirs differently. Ask which form and wording the engineer will supply.
6. **Which assumptions does your design rely on?** For footings and slabs this includes the AS 2870 site class and design wind speed, which councils ask the signatory to state.
7. **Do I need a soil test or a plumber's report first?** Some questions cannot be answered from a visit alone.
8. **What can't you tell me from a visual assessment?** A good engineer says plainly what stays hidden, such as what lies inside a cavity wall or under a slab.
9. **Who do I contract with, and who do I pay?** It should be the engineering firm, directly.
10. **Do you recommend a builder or underpinning contractor?** Be careful if the person assessing your problem also profits from the repair.

## Three jobs, and what the right engineer looks like for each

The scenarios below are anonymised composites of common Perth situations, not client stories. They show how the questions above change the answer.

### A double-brick wall in Duncraig

Take a 1970s double-brick home in Duncraig where the owners want the kitchen wall gone for an open-plan living area. In full-masonry houses, the inner leaf of the external walls and at least some internal walls usually carry ceilings, floors and roof, according to CSIRO. So the wall could be doing real work.

Removing or altering an element that supports another element is not exempt from a building permit under the Building Regulations 2012, Schedule 4. The owners need a beam design, and a building surveyor will want the engineer's calculations. What they should ask is question 3: does the quote cover the beam design only, or also the drawings the permit needs, and any follow-up?

The government charges are the part you can price. As a worked example, a certified permit costs 0.19% of the estimated value, but not less than $121.00. On a $30,000 job, 0.19% is $57, so the $121.00 minimum applies. The Building Services Levy for a job of $45,000 or under is $61.65. The engineer's fee is separate and is quoted per job. Our page on [wall removal engineering](/wall-removal-engineering/) covers the process, and the guide to [what a structural engineer does](/blog/what-does-a-structural-engineer-do/) explains the roles in the chain.

Nothing in this scenario says the wall can come out. That depends on what the engineer finds above and below it.

### A stepped crack in a Rockingham house

Now consider a stepped crack above a window in a Rockingham house two streets from the beach. The owners' first thought is a building inspection, because those have published prices in the $440 to $550 range.

CSIRO's damage categories give a rough scale. Category 1 is very slight, under 1 mm. Category 2 is slight, under 5 mm, with doors and windows sticking slightly. Category 3 is moderate, 5 to 15 mm, and pipes can fracture. Category 4 is severe, 15 to 25 mm. CSIRO also says most cracking in masonry is a cosmetic nuisance, and that monitoring should always be treated seriously. A crack width alone does not tell you the cause.

Near the coast, a rusting steel lintel is one possible cause: rust expands and cracks the brickwork around it, typically above windows and doors. A leaking pipe or poor drainage is another. Sorting those out is a diagnosis question, so the owners want an engineer's assessment, not only a defects list. The question to ask is number 8: what can a visual assessment not tell us? Our guide to [the structural engineer's report](/blog/structural-engineer-report/) explains what one contains, and the [structural inspections page](/structural-inspections/) describes the service.

### A gable patio in Baldivis

Finally, a gable patio in Baldivis that the council will not permit without an engineer's certificate. A freestanding Class 10a building is exempt only up to 10 square metres and 2.4 m high, and the pergola exemption covers only structures without a roof. A roofed patio does not fit the pergola item, so it is not covered by that exemption.

This is where the standard-versus-site-specific question bites. Standard certified steel patio drawings sell online for A$330, with a maximum truss span of 8.5 m. A site-specific design bases the slab and footings on your soil and includes wind calculations to AS/NZS 1170.2. No Perth firm publishes a price for the site-specific version. Ask the council first whether it will accept a standard detail on your block, because that is not guaranteed. Our guide to the [certificate of structural adequacy in WA](/blog/certificate-of-structural-adequacy-wa/) explains the wording councils use.

## Red flags worth taking seriously

Some warning signs are easy to spot once you know what registration and reporting should look like.

- **A guaranteed outcome.** Nobody can promise a permit will be approved, or that a wall can safely come out, before seeing it.
- **No registration number when asked.** Before 1 July 2027 this may have an explanation. After it, it is a problem.
- **A quote that bundles the repair.** Engineers assess and design. Registered builders build. If one party does both, ask how the advice stays independent.
- **A "certificate" with no drawings or scope listed.** A certificate should list the documents it covers, as the City of Perth form does.
- **A fixed price given over the phone with no photos or plans.** It may be honest, but ask what changes the price.
- **Copy that says "our engineers" on a site that only takes enquiries.** Be clear about who is actually doing the work.

## How to compare two quotes fairly

Once you have two or three quotes, line them up on the same headings. A cheaper quote that leaves out the drawings can end up costing more than a higher one that includes them.

Compare these things, in order:

- **Scope:** visit, report, calculations, drawings, certificate, council responses.
- **Registration:** class and number, checked on the register.
- **Form:** whether the certificate will match your council's own wording.
- **Assumptions:** site class, wind speed and anything the engineer says it cannot verify.
- **Payment terms:** who you pay, and when.

Ask each firm for a written quote before you commit. Where a timeline matters, get it in writing too, and expect it to depend on the job. If your reason for calling is a crack that has grown since you last looked, say so up front, because that changes how urgent the visit should be.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a structural engineer, or is a building inspector enough?

It depends on the question you are asking. A building inspector reports on visible defects and is usually enough for a pre-purchase condition check. If you need a beam designed, the cause of movement worked out, or a certificate for a permit, you need an engineer. The [structural inspections page](/structural-inspections/) explains where the line falls.

### How much does a residential structural engineer cost in Perth?

No Perth firm publishes a fee for a crack report, beam design or site-specific certificate, so engineers quote each job. The price depends on the house, the structure and what the council needs. The figures that are published, from $440 for a building inspection or A$330 for standard patio drawings, are for different products.

### Does the engineer have to be registered in WA?

Not yet for every job, but that is changing. Registration of structural building engineers began on 1 July 2024, with a transition period to 1 July 2027. After that date, structural engineering work must be done by a registered building engineer. You can read how the scheme works on the [WA Government registration page](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/multi-step-guides/building-engineering-registration).

### Can an engineer tell me a crack is harmless over the phone?

No, and you should be wary of anyone who does. CSIRO notes that most masonry cracking is a cosmetic nuisance, but that monitoring should always be treated seriously, and the cause matters more than the width. The [CSIRO foundation maintenance guide](https://research.csiro.au/infratech/wp-content/uploads/sites/38/2024/12/2979_FoundationMaintenanceandFootingPerformance_WCAG.pdf) explains the damage categories.
