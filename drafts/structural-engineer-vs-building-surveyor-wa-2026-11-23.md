# Building Surveyor vs Structural Engineer in WA: Who Does What on a Building Job

A home owner planning a wall removal or an extension in Perth is often asked to find a building surveyor and a structural engineer, sometimes by different people, sometimes in the wrong order. It is a fair question to ask what each one actually does and why the paperwork wants both. The short version: the surveyor decides whether the design complies with the building rules, the engineer tests and designs the structure, and the builder builds it. This guide explains how the Building Act 2011 (WA) sets those roles up.

## The surveyor's job: design compliance

Under the Building Act, a building permit is decided on the basis of a certificate of design compliance, form BA3, signed by a building surveyor. The surveyor is looking at the application as a whole against the building code and the Act. They are not designing your house.

There are two routes, and who the surveyor is depends on which one you use.

- **Certified application (BA1).** Under section 14, a certified application can be made for a building of any classification. Section 16 requires it to include a certificate of design compliance signed by a building surveyor. For this route the City of Rockingham explains that the BA3 comes from a registered private building surveying contractor.
- **Uncertified application (BA2).** Under section 14, uncertified applications are limited to classifications set by regulation, and regulation 15 prescribes Class 1a and Class 10. Under section 17 the permit authority refers the plans to a building surveyor, who decides whether to sign the certificate of design compliance. In the City of Rockingham's explanation, the council's own building surveyors assess it and issue the BA3 with the permit.

The two routes have different decision periods: under regulation 21 of the Building Regulations 2012, 10 business days for certified and 25 business days for uncertified, unless a longer time is agreed in writing. The clock can stop when more information is requested.

For the certificate itself, see [certificate of design compliance in WA](/blog/certificate-of-design-compliance-wa/).

## The engineer's job: the structure

A surveyor is not expected to calculate whether a steel beam will carry a roof. That is where the technical certificate comes in. The Building Act defines a "technical certificate" in section 3, and section 16(g) requires a certified application to include each technical certificate the surveyor relied on. In practice a technical certificate is signed by a specialist, and for structure that is typically a structural engineer.

What the engineer does depends on the job:

- assesses the existing structure, such as walls, footings, slab, roof and floors
- designs the new elements, such as a beam to replace a wall, footings for an extension, or a patio frame
- documents the design on drawings, and signs a certificate or letter the surveyor can rely on

Councils use their own names for that certificate. The Act speaks of a technical certificate; terms such as "certificate of structural adequacy" are not a statutory WA form name. One example is the City of Perth's form titled "Certificate of Structural Sufficiency". The signatory states the site class, the design wind speed, which documents the certificate covers, and confirms they are qualified as a professional engineer under the building code. Our guide to [certificate of structural adequacy in WA](/blog/certificate-of-structural-adequacy-wa/) goes through this.

## Where the builder fits

The builder carries out the work, to the approved plans and the engineer's design. A builder may help organise the application, and some builders offer packages that include engineering and permits. If someone offers both design and building in one package, ask who takes responsibility for which part.

## A third certificate: BA18

When work has already been built without approval, the route to regularise it is a building approval certificate, form BA13. One Perth firm describes the steps as council, a planning check, the BA13, a registered building surveyor who issues the BA18 certificate of building compliance, and a structural engineer's inspection and certification. So even there the surveyor and the engineer do separate jobs, and both are needed. The fee for a building approval certificate for unauthorised work is 0.38% of the estimated value, not less than $121.00, as set by the regulations. As an illustration only: on $40,000 of work, 0.38% would be $152.

Get the details of the process from your council. The state government page on [building approvals](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/building-and-energy/building-approvals) lists the current fees.

## Registration is changing for engineers

Registration of structural building engineers in Western Australia began on 1 July 2024. A transition period applies until 1 July 2027, after which structural building engineering work must be done by a registered building engineer, and unregistered work is an offence with a fine of up to $25,000. If an engineer lodges an application before 1 January 2027 and it is still undetermined on 1 July 2027, the engineer is taken to be registered. Building surveyors and builders are already registered under the Building Services (Registration) Act 2011.

The prescribed titles are specific. "Engineer" and "structural engineer" are not on the list of titles restricted by regulation, so ask any engineer you are considering about their registration status and class. The state guide is at [wa.gov.au building engineering registration](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/multi-step-guides/building-engineering-registration).

## What this costs

No Perth engineering firm we checked publishes an engineer's fee for a design or report. Engineers quote each job; the price depends on the house, the structure and what the council needs. Surveyor fees are not published in our sources either.

The government charges are published. A certified permit costs 0.19% of estimated value, not less than $121.00, and an uncertified permit 0.32%, not less than $121.00. The Building Services Levy is $61.65 on work of $45,000 or less, and 0.137% over that. As an illustration only: on $60,000 of work, 0.19% would be $114, so the $121.00 minimum applies. Check current figures with the council, as fees change.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a building surveyor sign off structural work instead of an engineer?

The surveyor signs the certificate of design compliance, and the Building Act lets them rely on technical certificates signed by specialists. For structural design, that specialist is typically a structural engineer. Ask your surveyor what they need for your job.

### What is the difference between a BA3 and a BA18?

The BA3 is the certificate of design compliance, signed by a building surveyor, for a building permit application. The BA18 is the certificate of building compliance a registered building surveyor issues when work already built is being regularised. They sit at different stages.

### Do I need a registered engineer now?

Registration began on 1 July 2024 and a transition period applies until 1 July 2027. After that date structural building engineering work must be done by a registered building engineer. Ask any engineer about their registration status.

### Is a builder allowed to do the engineering?

Engineers assess and design, and builders build. Some builders offer packages that include engineering. If you are offered one, ask who is responsible for what and who signs the certificate.
