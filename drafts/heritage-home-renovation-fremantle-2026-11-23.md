# Heritage Home Renovation in Fremantle: What to Settle Before the Structure Is Touched

A Fremantle owner who buys an old limestone or brick house and wants to open up the back, replace a verandah or fix a wall that has started to lean runs into two sets of rules at once. One set is about what the building is allowed to look like. The other is about whether the walls, footings and roof can carry what you want to do. They are decided by different people, and they can pull in different directions. This guide explains how the two fit together, so you ask the right questions in the right order.

## Why Fremantle is a different kind of renovation

The City of Fremantle says it protects around 2,500 sites, and it divides much of the older city into 23 heritage areas, including Central, North and South Fremantle and Hilton. The City's page also says development at a listed place requires planning approval. Its Heritage List follows a Council decision made in August 2025.

That wording matters. Planning approval is a separate step from a building permit. A house can be listed, or sit inside a heritage area, and the planning question is separate from the building one. The City's own heritage page is the place to check whether your address is listed or inside an area: [City of Fremantle heritage listings and areas](https://www.fremantle.wa.gov.au/planning-and-building/heritage/heritage-listings-and-areas/).

We have not checked the heritage lists of other western suburbs councils, so nothing here should be read as describing Cottesloe, Claremont, Nedlands or Mosman Park. Check your own council.

Kings Park Structural Co cannot tell you what a heritage officer will or will not approve. Nobody outside that process can. What we can do is explain the structural questions that tend to sit underneath the heritage ones.

## Planning approval and building permit are two doors

For a house in Western Australia, the local government is the permit authority for building work. In plain terms:

- Planning approval asks whether the change is acceptable for the place, its streetscape and its heritage significance.
- A building permit asks whether the work is structurally sound and complies with the building code.

Some building work is exempt from a permit. The exemptions sit in Schedule 4 of the Building Regulations 2012. For repair work, item 2(g) removes the exemption where the work is under a heritage protection order, a heritage works permit, a repair notice or a repair order. So a repair that might otherwise be exempt can fall back into needing a permit once one of those instruments applies.

Do not guess which side of that line your job falls on. Ask the City of Fremantle, or a registered building surveyor, before anyone starts. The same Schedule 4 rules also catch work that changes floor area or height, replaces footings, or removes an element that supports another element, regardless of heritage status.

## What is different about old masonry

Many heritage houses in Perth and Fremantle are masonry: limestone, brick, or a mix. CSIRO's guidance on foundation maintenance and footing performance makes a point that matters for all of them. Older masonry has little resistance to movement. Cracks show up as stepped lines through the mortar, and as vertical cracks at openings.

Three things follow from that.

First, an old wall that has moved a little over a century is not necessarily in trouble. CSIRO says most cracking found in masonry walls is a cosmetic nuisance. It also says monitoring should always be treated seriously. Both statements are true, and an engineer's job is to work out which one applies to your wall.

Second, old walls are rarely uniform. Lime mortar, rubble infill, later patching and added openings can all sit in the same wall. What you see on the surface may not describe what is inside.

Third, the cause of movement can be quite ordinary. CSIRO lists leaking water, sewer or stormwater pipes, downpipes that dump water beside the footing, over-watering from garden systems, and tree roots. In old houses these have usually had decades to act.

## Internal walls in older double-brick homes

A heritage renovation often starts with a wish to open up the floor plan. In full-masonry houses, CSIRO says the inner leaf of the external walls and at least some internal walls usually carry upper floors, ceilings and roof. So the wall you want to remove may be holding something up.

Removing or altering a supporting element is outside the building permit exemption. That is true for any house. In a heritage house it comes with an extra layer, because the planning side may also have a view on changes to internal layout, depending on how the place is listed. The two assessments do not replace each other.

## A sensible order of steps

The order below is about avoiding wasted design money. It is not a legal checklist.

1. **Find out how the place is protected.** Ask the City whether the address is on the Heritage List or inside one of the 23 heritage areas. Get the answer in writing if you can.
2. **Document the building as it is.** Photograph cracks with a tape measure in frame, note dates, and record sticking doors and windows.
3. **Rule out water.** Leaking pipes, broken stormwater connections and garden watering are cheaper to fix than walls.
4. **Get an engineer to look at the structure before the design is fixed.** If the walls or footings cannot take the plan, it is better to learn that before the architectural drawings are finished.
5. **Check approvals in order.** Planning approval, then the building permit route, with the building surveyor told about the heritage status from the start.
6. **Only then get builder quotes**, all based on the same engineer's scope.

Engineers assess and design. Registered builders build. We do not do either, and we do not arrange building work.

## What the government charges, and what is not published

No Perth engineering firm we checked publishes a fee for a structural assessment or a beam design. Engineers quote each job; the price depends on the house, the structure and what the council needs.

The government charges are published. They apply only if a building permit is needed.

| Item | Charge | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Certified building permit, Class 1 or 10 | 0.19% of estimated value, not less than $121.00 | Value includes GST |
| Uncertified building permit | 0.32% of estimated value, not less than $121.00 | Assessed by the council's surveyors |
| Building Services Levy on a permit | 0.137% if over $45,000; $61.65 if $45,000 or less | State levy |
| Construction Training Fund levy | 0.2% of the value, only where work is over $100,000 | Includes GST |

As an illustration only: on $60,000 of work, 0.19% would be $114, so the $121.00 minimum applies. Fees change by regulation, so confirm the current figures with the council.

A certified application goes to a private building surveyor, and the regulations set a 10 business day decision period; an uncertified application is assessed by the council, with 25 business days. The clock can stop if more information is requested, so ask the City how planning approval fits around those periods for your job.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need planning approval to renovate a heritage-listed house in Fremantle?

The City of Fremantle says development at a listed place requires planning approval. Whether your particular work counts as development, and what the City will want to see, is a question for the City. Ask before you commission drawings.

### Does a heritage listing change whether I need a building permit?

It can. Schedule 4 of the Building Regulations 2012 exempts some repair work from a permit, but item 2(g) removes that exemption for work under a heritage protection order, heritage works permit, repair notice or repair order. Confirm your situation with the City or a building surveyor.

### Is a crack in an old limestone or brick wall a sign of serious trouble?

Not necessarily. CSIRO says most cracking in masonry walls is a cosmetic nuisance, but also that monitoring should always be treated seriously. The width, pattern and whether it is still moving all matter, and only an on-site look can say.

### Can an engineer promise that my heritage renovation will be approved?

No. An engineer assesses the structure and designs the work. Planning and building decisions are made by the City and the building surveyor, and nobody can guarantee their outcome.
