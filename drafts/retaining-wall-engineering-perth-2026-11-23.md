# Retaining Wall Engineering in Perth: When You Need a Permit and an Engineer

You want to level a sloping back yard, or the limestone wall the previous owner built is leaning, and someone has said you need "engineering" for it. Nobody has said how much, or when. This guide explains the rule that decides whether a retaining wall needs a building permit in Western Australia, the difference between a standard certified detail and a site-specific design, and what actually drives the engineering. It will not tell you a particular wall is fine. That depends on the soil, the height and the water behind it.

## What a retaining wall has to resist

A retaining wall holds soil at two different levels. The soil on the high side pushes sideways on the wall all the time. Water in that soil pushes harder. Anything sitting on the high side adds to the push: a driveway, a shed, a swimming pool, a parked car, or a neighbour's house close to the boundary.

The wall resists in one of two ways. A gravity wall, such as a stack of limestone blocks or heavy concrete blocks, relies on its own weight and its base width. A cantilever or embedded wall, such as steel posts set in concrete with panels between them, relies on the posts being held in the ground below the low side. Both fail the same way when overloaded: they tilt, slide or bulge outward, and cracks open along the joints or the top.

A neat wall with no drainage can be less safe than a rough one with a proper drain behind it.

## The permit rule: 0.5 metres

Under the Building Regulations 2012 (WA), Schedule 4, item 6, a retaining wall is exempt from a building permit if it retains no more than 0.5 m and is not associated with other building work. Both conditions matter.

"Retains no more than 0.5 m" is about the height of soil held back, not the height of the wall you can see. "Not associated with other building work" means a small wall built as part of an extension, a new pool or a carport slab is not automatically exempt just because it is low. Read the two conditions together before deciding.

Above 0.5 m, the exemption does not apply. The wall goes through the normal permit process, and the building surveyor assessing the application will want engineering for it. What the surveyor needs, and in what form, differs by council and by job, so ask early. Your local government is the permit authority for houses, so it is the best first call.

The exemption only removes the need for a permit. It does not make a poorly built low wall safe, and it says nothing about boundaries, planning rules or stormwater. If you are unsure whether your wall is "associated with other building work", ask a building surveyor or your council's building team, not a landscaper.

## Limestone and block walls

Limestone, concrete block, sleeper and masonry retaining walls are all used in Perth, and the coastal strip sits on Tamala Limestone, which Geoscience Australia describes as calcareous dune rock with numerous solution channels and cavities. The type of wall changes the engineering questions.

| Wall type | How it resists the soil | What the engineer looks at |
|---|---|---|
| Limestone block | Own weight and base width | Block size, bonding between courses, base preparation, drainage |
| Concrete or masonry block | Weight, plus steel and grout in the cores | Reinforcing, footing, drainage, height |
| Steel posts and sleepers | Posts embedded in footings | Post spacing, footing depth, sleeper strength |

For a limestone wall the engineer will want to know what the blocks sit on, because gravity walls move if the base moves, and whether water can get out from behind the wall.

## Standard certified details versus a site-specific design

There are two routes to engineering for a retaining wall, and they are not the same thing.

**Standard certified details.** At least one Perth seller publishes pre-made drawings for common wall types, signed by an engineer. One Perth seller lists limestone, block and panel retaining walls at A$275.00 each (GST not stated). Its limestone retaining wall detail is limited to a height of 1800 mm. Those details are drawn for a generic situation, not for your block.

**Site-specific design.** An engineer looks at your wall: its height, the soil, the slope above, what is sitting on top and where the water goes. The design is drawn for that site. Engineers quote each job; the price depends on the house, the structure and what the council needs. No Perth engineering firm we checked publishes a fee for this.

Whether a particular council will accept a standard detail for a particular wall is not something we could find stated anywhere, so we cannot say it will. If you are tempted to buy a standard detail, ask the permit authority first whether it will accept one for your site. Buying the drawing and finding out afterwards is the expensive order.

A standard detail is built on assumptions about soil, slope and load behind the wall. If any of those do not match your yard, the detail may not apply.

## What drives the design

An engineer designing a retaining wall works through a short list of inputs. Each one changes the answer.

### Soil

Sand drains well but does not hold a steep face, and CSIRO notes that sandy soil is particularly susceptible to being washed away. Clay holds water and swells and shrinks with the seasons, which loads a wall differently through the year. Geoscience Australia describes brown silty clay in the Guildford Formation across much of the eastern Perth region, and colluvial slopes at the foot of the Darling Scarp. Nobody can tell you what is under your block from a map. A site-specific design starts from what the soil actually is.

### Surcharge

Surcharge is the extra load on the soil behind the wall. A driveway, a pool, a shed or a house footing within a short distance of the top of the wall adds load the soil would not otherwise carry. A garden wall with a lawn behind it and a retaining wall holding up a carport slab are different problems, even at the same height.

### Drainage behind the wall

This is the item most often missed. Water that cannot escape builds pressure behind the wall, and that pressure can exceed the soil's own push. Drainage usually means free-draining material against the back face and a way to carry the water out. How much, and where, depends on the site.

### Height

Doubling the height of a wall does much more than double the load on it, which is why a 1.5 m wall is a different category of work from a 0.5 m garden wall.

## Walls on or near a boundary

The excavation for a boundary wall's footing can undermine a neighbour's fence, path or footing, the wall changes how water moves across the boundary, and a failure puts soil on someone else's land.

This article cannot give you the planning or fencing rules that apply. Your council can. An engineer addresses the structural side: how deep to dig and how to support the neighbour's side during the work.

## Soakwells and downpipes near the wall

Stormwater is a common hidden load on a retaining wall. A soakwell or an unconnected downpipe that discharges near the high side puts water straight into the soil behind the wall. CSIRO notes that downpipes not connected to a stormwater system concentrate water next to footings, and the same logic applies to a wall.

If you have a soakwell uphill of a retaining wall, tell whoever is designing the wall. The City of Joondalup, for example, says a soakwell should sit at least its own width away from buildings and boundaries, so the excavation does not undermine footings, fences or walls. We could not find other councils' distances, so check with your own council.

## Who does what

Engineers assess and design walls. Builders build them.

Structural engineering in Western Australia is being brought under registration. Registration of building engineers began on 1 July 2024, and after a transition period ending 1 July 2027, structural building engineering work must be done by a registered building engineer. Ask any engineer who quotes you for their registration status and check it yourself on the [building engineering registration page on wa.gov.au](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/multi-step-guides/building-engineering-registration).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a permit for a retaining wall in Perth?

Not if the wall retains no more than 0.5 m and is not associated with other building work, under Schedule 4, item 6 of the Building Regulations 2012. Above that height, or when the wall is part of other building work, the exemption does not apply. Confirm with your council or a building surveyor before you start.

### Can I buy a standard retaining wall detail instead of a site-specific design?

At least one Perth seller publishes standard certified details for limestone and block walls, listed at A$275.00 each, with its limestone detail limited to 1800 mm. Whether your council will accept a standard detail for your site is not something we found stated, so ask the permit authority before you buy.

### What makes a retaining wall fail?

Usually water that cannot drain from behind it, extra load on the high side, soil that is weaker than assumed, or a base that moves. Height multiplies all of these. A wall that leans, bulges or cracks along its joints should be looked at by an engineer rather than patched.

### How much does a retaining wall engineer cost?

No Perth engineering firm we checked publishes a fee for a site-specific design. Engineers quote each job, and the price depends on the house, the structure and what the council needs. Ask for a written quote that says what is included.
