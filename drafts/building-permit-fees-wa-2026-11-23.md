# Building Permit Fees in WA: What the Government Charges and How to Work It Out

You have a rough figure for the work, a council fee sheet open in one tab, and a wa.gov.au page in another, and the numbers do not agree. That can happen because older council sheets still show figures that have since been replaced. This guide sets out the current Western Australian permit fees, the two levies that sit on top, and how to run the sums yourself for a particular job.

It covers government charges only. Engineer fees and builder prices are separate, and they are covered near the end.

## The permit fees themselves

The fees come from Schedule 2 of the Building Regulations 2012, as inserted by SL 2026/95. The wa.gov.au building approvals page (last updated 4 August 2026) shows the same table.

| Application | Fee |
|---|---|
| Certified building permit, Class 1 or Class 10 or incidental structure | 0.19% of estimated value, but not less than $121.00 |
| Uncertified building permit | 0.32% of estimated value, not less than $121.00 |
| Demolition permit, Class 1 or 10 | $121.00 |
| Building approval certificate, unauthorised work (s 51) | 0.38% of estimated value, minimum $121.00 |
| Building approval certificate, existing building (s 52) | $121.00 |

Two things to take from the table. First, the percentage only matters once the work is large enough to beat the minimum. At 0.19%, the certified rate stays below $121.00 until the value of work passes about $63,700. Second, the uncertified route costs more in percentage terms than the certified one. Certified and uncertified are the two application routes under the Building Act 2011: a certified application arrives with a certificate of design compliance signed by a private building surveyor, while on an uncertified application the local government refers the plans to a building surveyor. If the difference between them is new to you, [the certificate of design compliance explained](/blog/certificate-of-design-compliance-wa/) sets out what each route involves.

Uncertified applications are only available for Class 1a and Class 10 buildings. A certified application can be made for a building of any classification.

## What counts as the "value of work"

The percentage is applied to the estimated value of the work, and that value is worked out including GST. If a builder's quote is shown as ex-GST, add the GST before you apply any of the rates in this article. Leaving it out is an easy way to under-estimate a fee.

The estimate is a figure given on the application, and the permit authority can query it. This guide cannot tell you how a particular council tests the number, so ask the council if the work is unusual.

## The two levies on top

Two state levies are charged separately from the permit fee.

**Building Services Levy.** For a building or demolition permit, the levy is 0.137% of the value if the value is over $45,000. If the value is $45,000 or less, it is a flat $61.65. For a building approval certificate for unauthorised work, the levy is 0.274% of the value if over $45,000, or $123.30 if $45,000 or less.

**Construction Training Fund levy.** The CTF levy is 0.2% of the value of construction work, including GST, and it is payable only where the value is more than $100,000. The threshold was $20,000 before 1 July 2026. If you read a council sheet that says $20,000, it is out of date. The Fund's own [CTF levy page](https://ctf.wa.gov.au/levy-disbursements-guide/the-ctf-levy) is the place to check it. Older sheets also call it the BCITF, which is the previous name for the same levy.

## Illustrative calculations

These are arithmetic on the government percentages above, shown so you can see how the pieces combine. They are illustrations only. They are not quotes, not predictions of what any council will charge, and the project values are made up for the sums.

**A certified permit on $40,000 of work.** 0.19% of $40,000 is $76, which is below the minimum, so the permit fee is $121.00. The value is under $45,000, so the Building Services Levy is the flat $61.65. The value is under $100,000, so no CTF levy. The three charges add up to $182.65.

**A certified permit on $60,000 of work.** 0.19% of $60,000 is $114, so the $121.00 minimum applies again. The value is now over $45,000, so the Building Services Levy is 0.137% of $60,000, which is $82.20. No CTF levy. Total: $203.20.

**A certified permit on $250,000 of work.** 0.19% of $250,000 is $475.00. The Building Services Levy is 0.137%, or $342.50. The CTF levy applies because the value is over $100,000, at 0.2%, which is $500.00. Total: $1,317.50.

**The same $250,000 on an uncertified application.** 0.32% of $250,000 is $800.00, so the permit fee rises by $325.00 compared with the certified route. The two levies are unchanged on these figures, so the total would be $1,642.50.

**A building approval certificate for unauthorised work valued at $50,000.** 0.38% of $50,000 is $190.00. The levy for this category is 0.274% because the value is over $45,000, which is $137.00. Total: $327.00. The certificate for unauthorised work costs more than a normal permit on the same value, and the levy rate is double.

Notice how much the minimums matter on smaller jobs. In the first two illustrations the $121.00 floor and the levy make up the whole bill, and the percentage rate never comes into it.

## The stale-figure trap

Council websites and old PDFs are where owners get caught. The City of Stirling's building fees sheet, effective 1 July 2021, still shows a $110.00 minimum and a $20,000 threshold for the training levy under its old BCITF name. The current regulation minimum is $121.00, and the CTF levy threshold is $100,000. That sheet is one example. If any page you find shows $110, treat it as superseded and check the figure against the wa.gov.au [building approvals page](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/building-and-energy/building-approvals) before you budget.

The same habit applies to anything else with a date on it. Fees are set by regulation, they change, and a document that says "with effect from" a past year was correct for that year only.

## What the government charges do not include

The fees above are government charges. They leave out:

- **The engineer's fee.** No Perth engineering firm we checked publishes a price. Engineers quote each job. The price depends on the house, the structure and what the council needs. For a fuller breakdown of what is and is not published, see [what a structural engineer costs in Perth](/structural-engineer-cost-perth/).
- **The building surveyor's fee**, on a certified application. We have no published figure for it and will not guess.
- **The builder's price** for the work itself.
- **Home indemnity insurance**, which the builder arranges. Residential building work over $20,000 must be covered.

The engineer's output is also not the permit. A structural engineer designs and certifies the structural part, and the building surveyor or the council decides the application. For an example of how that plays out on a common job, see [wall removal and beam design](/wall-removal-engineering/).

## Do you need a permit at all?

Some work is exempt. Under Schedule 4 of the Building Regulations 2012, a renovation is exempt only if it does not affect structural soundness and does not involve a change in floor area or height, underpinning or replacement of footings, or removal or alteration of an element that supports another element. So taking out a load-bearing wall, adding a room or reworking footings is not exempt, and the fees above come into play. Whether your job needs a permit is a question for your builder, your building surveyor or your local council.

## A short checklist before you budget

1. Get the estimated value of the work including GST.
2. Decide which application route applies: certified or uncertified.
3. Apply the percentage and compare it with the $121.00 minimum.
4. Add the Building Services Levy: the flat amount at $45,000 or under, or the percentage above it.
5. Add the CTF levy only if the value is over $100,000.
6. Confirm every figure against a current source, dated 2026 or later.
7. Keep the engineer's fee and the builder's price as separate lines.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the minimum building permit fee in WA?

The minimum for a certified or an uncertified building permit is $121.00, and the same figure applies to a demolition permit for a Class 1 or Class 10 building. The building approval certificate for an existing building under section 52 is also $121.00. Older council sheets that show $110.00 are out of date.

### Does the permit fee include GST?

The permit fee is calculated on the estimated value of the work, and that value includes GST. So if your builder's quote is ex-GST, add the GST first and then apply the percentage. The same GST-inclusive value is used for the CTF levy.

### When does the CTF levy apply?

The Construction Training Fund levy is 0.2% of the value of construction work, including GST, and it is payable only where the value is more than $100,000. The threshold was $20,000 before 1 July 2026, so sheets quoting the lower figure are out of date.

### Is the engineer's fee part of the permit fee?

No. The permit fee and the two levies are government charges. The engineer is engaged separately, quotes each job, and the price depends on the house, the structure and what the council needs.
