# Cracks in a Concrete Slab: Shrinkage or Movement?

You spot a crack running across the garage floor, or a line under the lounge-room tiles that wasn't there last winter, and the question arrives straight away: is the house moving, or has the concrete simply done what concrete does? Two very different answers, and one photo rarely settles it.

Cracks in a concrete slab fall into two broad families. Some come from the concrete drying and shrinking and tend to sit still. Others come from the ground under the slab changing and tend to keep going. This guide covers what separates them, what to measure, which Perth conditions make movement likelier, and when you need an independent structural engineer rather than a search engine.

It won't tell you your crack is harmless. Nobody honestly can from a web page.

## Why cracks in a concrete slab are so common

Concrete is strong when you squash it and weak when you stretch it. It also shrinks as it dries. A slab that is held back by its edges, its reinforcement or the ground beneath it can't shrink freely, so it relieves the strain by cracking. That is shrinkage, and it is a normal, well-understood part of how concrete behaves.

First work out which slab you're looking at. The house slab carries the walls, so a crack there can matter to everything above it. A garage, shed or patio slab is often poured separately and lightly loaded. Paths and driveways are mostly about trip hazards and drainage. If you can't tell, or the crack runs from the slab into a wall, treat it as the house slab.

If walls or ceilings are cracking too, read [when cracks in walls are worth worrying about](/blog/cracks-in-walls-when-to-worry/) alongside this page. A slab crack and a wall crack in the same part of the house are one story, not two.

## Shrinkage cracks versus movement cracks: how to tell them apart

No single sign proves it. What helps is the combination: the shape, when it showed up, whether it's changing and what else in the house is reacting. This table is a starting point for your own observations, not a diagnosis.

| What you see | Points towards shrinkage | Points towards movement |
|---|---|---|
| Width | Hairline, stays the same over months | Widens, or is wider at one end |
| Pattern | Fine random or map-like lines, or straight lines running to corners and openings | One long crack, often with a matching crack in a wall above |
| Height difference | Both sides level | One side sits higher than the other |
| Timing | Appeared soon after the slab was poured | Appeared after a wet winter, a leak, a tree or new work nearby |
| Rest of the house | Doors and windows work normally | Doors or windows stick, cornices or tile grout crack, brickwork cracks |
| Behaviour | Static | Opens and closes with the seasons, or keeps growing |

Two clues carry extra weight. First, **a step or lip across the crack**, where one edge is higher than the other. A level difference suggests the ground under one side has moved. Second, **cracks that line up**. If a slab crack sits directly below a stepped crack in a brick wall, or below a door that has started to bind, the two are probably related.

### What the crack widths tell you, and what they don't

CSIRO's guide to foundation maintenance (Building Technology File 18, 2024 edition) reproduces the damage categories from AS 2870, and engineers use them constantly. They're written for walls and finishes, not slabs, so treat them as a yardstick for how much the building is being affected.

- **Category 0, negligible:** cracks under 0.1 mm, hairlines.
- **Category 1, very slight:** under 1 mm, fine cracks that don't need repair.
- **Category 2, slight:** under 5 mm, noticeable and easily filled, with doors and windows sticking slightly.
- **Category 3, moderate:** 5 to 15 mm, or several cracks of 3 mm or more in one group. Doors and windows stick and pipes can fracture.
- **Category 4, severe:** 15 to 25 mm, with frames distorting and walls leaning or bulging.

A width on its own doesn't tell you whether a crack is finished or still growing, and it can't tell you why it happened. CSIRO notes that symptoms often don't simply reflect the original cause, which is why engineers look at the whole building rather than one line on the floor.

Measure it the way an engineer would want to hear about it. Note the width at its widest point, note the date, and photograph it with a ruler alongside. Repeat in a month or two from the same spot. A record of "unchanged" or "visibly wider" beats any guess.

## What moves a slab in Perth: sand, clay, water and roots

Whether the ground moves depends heavily on what you're built on and what's happening around the house. Geoscience Australia describes Bassendean Sand over much of central Perth, with Tamala Limestone and dune sands along the coastal strip. The Guildford Formation, a silty clay, outcrops over much of the eastern suburbs, colluvial slopes sit at the foot of the Darling Scarp, and the Pinjarra Plain around Peel is mainly clayey alluvium. A Perth geotechnical firm notes that sand is the most common soil in the metropolitan area and that clay sites turn up particularly in the Perth Hills.

That's a regional picture only. The AS 2870 site class is worked out for a specific block, not a suburb. What the regional picture does tell you is which failure mode to think about first.

**On sand, water is the culprit.** CSIRO says sandy soil is particularly prone to being washed away. A downpipe that empties onto the ground, a leaking stormwater or sewer pipe or a poorly placed soakwell can carry sand out from under a slab edge. The slab then loses support, sags or cracks, and the crack often sits near the source of the water. The City of Joondalup requires soakwells to sit at least their own width away from buildings, so the digging does not undermine footings.

**On clay, moisture is the culprit.** Reactive clay swells when wet and shrinks when dry. CSIRO describes the pattern: doming and dishing, doors binding, cornices cracking. Leaks matter more here too, and CSIRO's wording is that even a tiny leak can saturate a clay foundation.

**Trees and gardens.** Roots can lift footings or dry clay until it shrinks. Our guide to [tree roots and cracking houses](/blog/tree-roots-cracking-house/) covers the tree side. For the wider picture of why local houses move, see [why Perth houses crack](/blog/why-perth-houses-crack/).

## Three Perth slab cracks, and what an engineer would look at

These are anonymised, typical scenarios. None is a real client.

### A Kalamunda home on clay whose back doors started sticking

After a wet winter, the owners of a home in the Perth Hills notice the back door catching on the frame and a crack running across the family-room slab, visible where the carpet meets the tiles. The crack measures around 3 mm and, when re-measured in spring, looks wider at one end.

The Perth Hills is one of the areas where sources support clay sites, so seasonal swelling is a reasonable suspect, but only a suspect. On the CSIRO scale a 3 mm crack is category 2, though several cracks that size in one group reach category 3. An engineer would look at the slab, the walls above it, drainage, trees and plumbing, then say what is causing the movement and what should happen next. A leaking pipe under a clay slab is a very different problem from a tree.

### A Baldivis patio slab with fine cracks and a permit question

A family has a new slab poured for a gable patio. Within weeks it shows a network of hairline cracks. The bigger question is the permit. A roofed patio is not a pergola under the Building Regulations, which define a pergola as a structure with no roof, so a roofed patio doesn't fall under the exemption for pergolas of up to 20 m2.

The council or building surveyor may want an engineer's certificate (often called a certificate of structural adequacy, though the Building Act's term is a technical certificate). To illustrate the government charges only, take an estimated project value of $12,000. A certified building permit costs 0.19 per cent of the value, not less than $121.00, so the minimum applies. The Building Services Levy is $61.65 for work of $45,000 or less. That's a little under $183, before anyone's fee for the engineering. Engineers quote each job, so this is an example, not a quote.

Fine cracks on a new slab can be curing behaviour, but the person to say so is the engineer who reads the design and sees the concrete. Our [patio, carport and shed certificates page](/patio-shed-certificates/) explains what to have ready, and the blog post on the [engineering certificate for a patio or shed](/blog/engineering-certificate-patio-shed/) goes deeper on the paperwork.

### A Rockingham house near the coast, with a garage slab edge that's dropped

A home two streets from the beach has a garage slab crack running parallel to the front edge, with the outer strip sitting a few millimetres lower than the rest. A downpipe discharges onto bare ground at that corner.

That combination is the sand-and-water pattern. CSIRO warns that downpipes not connected to stormwater concentrate water next to footings, and lists fixing plumbing and drainage and a paved apron at least 900 mm wide with a fall of 1:60 as general remedies, not a prescription for this house. The engineer works out whether ground has been lost from under the slab and whether the footing is affected. Underpinning or replacing footings is not exempt from a building permit in WA, and a registered builder does it, not the engineer.

## Who should look at a cracked concrete slab, and what does it cost?

Start with the least you can do. Photograph, measure and date the crack. If the crack is hairline, unchanged and nothing else in the house is reacting, you may simply keep watching it. If it has a step, is widening, lines up with cracks in walls or has doors sticking, get someone qualified to look.

**Building inspector or structural engineer?** Many Perth "structural inspections" are AS 4349.1 pre-purchase building inspections by building inspectors. Published Perth prices for those run from about $440 incl. GST up to about $550, and at least one Perth firm says outright that it doesn't provide structural design, engineering drawings or engineering certification. They're useful when you're buying a house. When the question is why the slab moved and what it needs, you want an engineer. **What does it cost?** No Perth engineering firm we could find publishes a fee for a crack report. Engineers quote each job. The price depends on the house, how much of it needs looking at and what you need at the end, whether that's a letter, a report or design work. **Checking the engineer.** Registration of structural building engineers in WA began on 1 July 2024. From 1 July 2027, structural engineering must be done by a registered building engineer. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are hairline cracks in a concrete slab normal?

Hairline cracks are very common and often reflect the concrete shrinking as it dried. CSIRO's damage scale treats cracks under 0.1 mm as negligible. That said, a hairline that widens, sits above a wall crack or comes with sticking doors deserves a proper look. Measure it, date it and re-check in a month or two.

### How do I know if a slab crack means the house is moving?

Look for a step across the crack, a width that keeps growing, matching cracks in walls or ceilings, and doors or windows that stick. Any one of these can have other explanations, so they're clues, not proof. An engineer who has seen the whole house can tell you what's behind them.

### How much does an engineer's report on a cracked slab cost in Perth?

No Perth engineering firm we found publishes a fee. Engineers quote each job, and the price depends on the house, the extent of the problem and what you need. Perth prices of about $440 to $550 that you'll see advertised are mostly for building inspections, which are a different product.

### Can I just fill the crack myself?

Filling a crack hides it and doesn't tell you why it opened. If the cause is a leak or washed-out sand, it can reopen while the problem carries on. Find the cause first, and ask an engineer before filling anything that is more than hairline or changing.
