# New Home Cracks and the Builder in WA: What to Record, Who to Ask and How Disputes Work

A hairline crack above a door in a house that is only a year or two old is common enough, and it is still unsettling to find. The harder situation is when the cracks keep growing, or you start to wonder whether the builder will take them seriously. This guide covers the two questions that matter most: is the crack cosmetic or structural, and what is the Western Australian pathway if you and the builder cannot agree? It is general information, not legal advice.

## Start with what the crack is

Engineers and builders in Australia usually describe cracks using the damage categories in AS 2870, which CSIRO reproduces in its guide to foundation maintenance and footing performance. The widths below are approximate limits.

| Category | Approx. crack width | Typical damage |
|---|---|---|
| 0 Negligible | under 0.1 mm | Hairline cracks |
| 1 Very slight | under 1 mm | Fine cracks, not needing repair |
| 2 Slight | under 5 mm | Noticeable, easily filled; doors and windows may stick slightly |
| 3 Moderate | 5 to 15 mm, or several cracks of 3 mm or more in one group | Some wall replacement possible; doors and windows stick; pipes can fracture |
| 4 Severe | 15 to 25 mm, also depends on how many cracks | Extensive repair; frames distort; walls lean or bulge |

CSIRO's guide stops at category 4 in its current edition, so do not rely on tables you find elsewhere that add a fifth.

CSIRO also says most cracking found in masonry walls is a cosmetic nuisance only, and that monitoring should always be treated seriously. Those two statements sit side by side for a reason. A crack's width on the day tells you less than whether it is changing.

## Why a new house can crack

A new house is not immune to the causes CSIRO lists for older ones. Those include:

- clay soils that swell when wet and shrink when dry, which makes floors dish or dome and cracks cornices
- leaking water, sewer or stormwater pipes, where even a small leak can saturate a clay foundation
- downpipes that are not connected to a stormwater system, which concentrate roof water beside the footing
- over-watering from automatic garden systems
- trees whose roots lift footings or dry out clay

Several of these are things an owner can influence after handover, which is why a dispute can turn on whether the cause was the building work or the site's water. An engineer's report is the usual way to separate the two. ## Document before anything else

Whatever happens next, the best evidence you will ever have is a dated record made early. The point is to show whether the cracks are changing.

1. **Photograph each crack** with a tape measure or ruler in frame, plus a wide shot showing where it sits.
2. **Measure and date it.** Write the width and length on a list, and repeat monthly or after heavy rain.
3. **Note related symptoms**: doors or windows that bind, gaps opening at cornices or skirting, tiles cracking, floors that no longer feel level.
4. **Record water sources**: any leaks, wet patches, downpipe outlets, garden beds against the wall.
5. **Keep every written communication** with the builder, including the date you first reported each problem.
6. **Keep your contract, plans and handover paperwork** where you can find them.

Draw a pencil line at the end of a crack with a date, or stick a dated mark across it. If the crack has grown past the line next month, you have a fact rather than an impression.

## Talking to the builder first

Most disputes start as a conversation, and most of what follows is easier if you have already put your concern in writing. Send the builder an email that states what you have seen, includes your photos and dates, and asks what they propose to do and by when. Keep the tone factual. A builder who rectifies a problem under their contract has no reason to be in a dispute at all, and a polite written record helps if one develops.

Be careful about paying someone else to repair a crack before an independent person has recorded it. Once it is filled and painted, the evidence is gone.

## The independent engineer's report

An engineer's report can establish what the cracks are doing and what is likely driving them. Findings may cover the walls, floors, slab and footings, and cracking, sagging or shifting. ## The WA dispute pathway

Building and Energy, part of DEMIRS, handles building disputes under the Building Services (Complaint Resolution and Administration) Act 2011. The state government's page, last updated 25 June 2026, sets out the process: [wa.gov.au building dispute resolution](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/multi-step-guides/building-dispute-resolution). The points relevant to cracking are:

- **Building service complaints** can generally be made up to 6 years after the work was completed.
- **Home building work contract complaints**, for contracts valued from $7,500 to $500,000, are generally limited to 3 years.
- **A Notice of Proposed Complaint** must be served on the other party at least 14 days before you lodge a complaint.

The word "generally" is doing real work. Time limits can depend on details of your contract and what happened when. Read the government's page, and consider getting advice specific to your circumstances if the dates are close.

## Home indemnity insurance

Residential building work over $20,000 must be covered by home indemnity insurance, according to the state government. That is a builder's obligation, and it sits in the background of any dispute. We have not found the cover limits on a page we could verify, so we cannot say what the policy would pay. Ask your builder for the certificate of insurance, and read it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long do I have to make a complaint about building work in WA?

According to the state government's guide, building service complaints can generally be made up to 6 years after the work was completed. Home building work contract complaints, for contracts valued between $7,500 and $500,000, are generally limited to 3 years. Check the details against your own contract and dates.

### What is a Notice of Proposed Complaint?

It is a notice you serve on the other party before lodging a complaint with Building and Energy. It must be served at least 14 days before you lodge. The government's dispute resolution page explains the steps.

### Are hairline cracks in a new house a problem?

CSIRO's categories treat cracks under 0.1 mm as negligible and under 1 mm as very slight, needing no repair. It also says monitoring should always be treated seriously. Record the crack and see whether it changes.

### Does home indemnity insurance cover cracking?

We could not find verified cover limits, so we cannot say. The state government says residential building work over $20,000 must be covered. Ask your builder for the certificate and read what it covers.
