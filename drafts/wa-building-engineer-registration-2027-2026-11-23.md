# Structural Engineer Registration in WA: What Changes on 1 July 2027

If you are about to hire an engineer for a wall removal, an extension or a patio, you may have seen the words "registered building engineer" and wondered if you can ignore them until the law bites. You may also have seen 1 July 2026 quoted as the deadline, and then found 1 July 2027 somewhere else. This guide sets out what the government says, what is already in force, and what a home owner should ask for today.

This is general information, not legal advice. For anything that turns on the wording of the law, check the sources linked below or speak to a lawyer.

## The short version

Registration of structural and fire safety building engineers in Western Australia commenced on 1 July 2024. A transition period applies until 1 July 2027. After that date, doing building engineering work without being registered is an offence, with a fine of up to $25,000.

That is the position in the wa.gov.au guide on building engineering registration, last updated 17 August 2026. You can read it at the [building engineering registration page on wa.gov.au](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/multi-step-guides/building-engineering-registration).

## About the 1 July 2026 date you may have seen

Some older pages say structural registration becomes mandatory on 1 July 2026. That date is superseded. The consolidated regulations, the Building Services (Registration) Regulations 2011 as at 1 July 2026, list the relevant provisions as commencing on 1 July 2027. The government guide agrees. If a page you are reading gives 1 July 2026 as the mandatory date, it is out of date, and the date to use is 1 July 2027.

One thing did expire on 1 July 2026: a 50% early-bird discount on registration fees. That concerns engineers paying for registration, not you.

## What the transition period means

The transition period gives engineers time to apply and be assessed. It does not mean there is no regime in place now. Some provisions already apply, and one has a date.

**Already in force.** The Building Services (Registration) Act 2011 stops anyone using a title that the regulations prescribe unless they are registered in a class that entitles them to it, and stops them advertising or implying that they are registered when they are not. Each of those carries a fine of $25,000.

**From 1 July 2027.** On our reading, the provisions that stop unregistered people carrying out, or advertising that they will carry out, a prescribed building service begin to bite on building engineering work from that date. Until then the current regulations prescribe only builder, building surveying and painter work.

**The exception.** An application lodged before 1 January 2027 and still undetermined on 1 July 2027 is taken to be registered. So an engineer who has applied in time and is waiting on a decision is not caught by the offence on the changeover date. Applications lodged on or after 1 January 2027 do not get that cover.

The practical effect is a deadline for engineers. For a home owner, it is a reason to check status properly, not a reason to rush a job.

## Which titles are protected

This is where owners get confused. Regulation 28K of the Building Services (Registration) Regulations 2011 lists the prescribed titles. Among them are:

- Registered Building Engineer.
- Registered Building Engineering Practitioner: Structural, in Professional, Technologist and Associate versions. There are Fire Safety, Fire Systems and Civil equivalents.
- Registered Building Engineering Contractor, and the Professional, Technologist and Associate versions, as an individual, a partnership or a company.

"Engineer", "structural engineer" and "consulting engineer" are not in the regulation 28K list. Our reading, which is not legal advice, is that they are not restricted titles under the Act. So a person can call themselves a structural engineer without being on the register. That does not make the label meaningless. Those words are still subject to the Australian Consumer Law, which prohibits misleading claims. But it does mean the protected phrase to look for is the registered one, not the plain title.

## The Building Engineer Register

DEMIRS, the Department that includes Building and Energy, publishes a Building Engineer Register. It lists current contractors, shown with a BEC number, and practitioners, shown with a BEP number. If someone quotes you for structural work, you can look them up.

We have not re-read the register page for this article, so check its current layout and search options on the DEMIRS site rather than relying on our description of it.

## What this means if you are hiring now

On our reading, hiring an engineer who is not yet registered breaks no rule before 1 July 2027. Still, a transition period is a good time to build a habit. Here is a sensible way to use it.

1. **Ask for the registration class and number** when you get a quote. A firm that has registered will usually say so. A firm that has applied should be able to say when.
2. **Look the number up on the register** yourself. Do not rely on a logo or a line in an email.
3. **Ask who is signing the work.** The firm and the individual engineer can be separate entries on the register.
4. **Ask what the quote covers.** A site inspection and a design with a certificate are different jobs, and [what a structural engineer does](/blog/what-does-a-structural-engineer-do/) explains the split.
5. **Keep a written quote.** The price depends on the house, the structure and what the council needs. Engineers quote each job, and no Perth firm we checked publishes a fee.
6. **Plan for dates.** If your job will run past 1 July 2027, say so in your first conversation.

If you work through the checklist above, you will cover most of what [choosing a residential structural engineer](/blog/choosing-a-residential-structural-engineer/) recommends anyway.

## What registration does not tell you

Registration is a floor, not a recommendation. It does not tell you how good the engineer is, how fast they will reply, or whether their report suits your council's requirements. It also does not turn a builder's inspection into an engineer's report. And it does not guarantee a permit: building permits are decided by the building surveyor or local government, not by the register.

## Common mistakes to avoid

**Treating the deadline as already passed.** The mandatory date is 1 July 2027. Acting as if it were 2026 may lead you to turn away someone you are free to hire.

**Treating the deadline as irrelevant.** The title rules are in force now. If someone claims to be a "Registered Building Engineer" and is not on the register, that is a claim worth questioning, and the Act treats it seriously.

**Confusing registered with certified.** A certificate is a document an engineer signs. Registration is a status held on a register. Neither stands in for the other.

**Assuming one registration covers all work.** Classes differ. The structural class is separate from fire safety, fire systems and civil.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When does structural engineer registration become mandatory in WA?

Registration commenced on 1 July 2024, with a transition period until 1 July 2027. After that date, doing building engineering work without being registered is an offence with a fine of up to $25,000. Pages that give 1 July 2026 are out of date.

### Can I still hire an unregistered engineer before 1 July 2027?

During the transition period, building engineering is not yet a prescribed building service under the current regulations, so the restriction on carrying out the work does not yet apply. The rules on using protected titles and claiming to be registered do apply now. Check the status of anyone you hire, and read the government guide for the wording.

### Can anyone call themselves a structural engineer in WA?

The words engineer and structural engineer are not in the list of prescribed titles in regulation 28K. Our reading, which is not legal advice, is that they are not restricted under the Act. The registered titles, such as Registered Building Engineer, are restricted, and the Australian Consumer Law applies to misleading claims.

### How do I check an engineer's registration?

DEMIRS publishes a Building Engineer Register listing current contractors and practitioners. Ask the engineer for their registration class and number, then look them up on the register yourself rather than relying on a quote or website.
