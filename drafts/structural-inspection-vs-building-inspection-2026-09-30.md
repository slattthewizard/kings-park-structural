# Structural Inspection vs Building Inspection: Which One Do You Need in Perth?

A pre-purchase building report that starts from $440 including GST can tell you a wall has cracked. It will not tell you whether that wall is holding up your roof, and that gap is where a lot of Perth buyers and owners get caught out. If you are weighing a structural inspection against a building inspection, the right choice depends on the question you need answered, not on which name sounds more thorough.

The short version: a building inspection is a general condition check of a house, usually done before a purchase. A structural inspection is an engineer's assessment of how the walls, footings, slab and roof structure are carrying load and moving. ## Structural inspection vs building inspection: the plain difference

The two names get mixed up constantly, and Perth's market makes it worse. Many reports sold as "structural inspections" are pre-purchase building reports written to AS 4349.1 by building inspectors. One Perth firm says openly that its reports are prepared by licensed building inspectors. Another says in plain terms that it does not provide structural design, engineering drawings or engineering certification.

A building inspector is the right person for a general check of a house you might buy. The problem only starts when a building inspector's report is treated as an engineer's opinion on the frame.

So the first thing to check on any report is who signs it. Some inspection firms say their inspections are done by a registered builder or a structural engineer, which means the person behind two similar-looking reports can differ. Ask which one you are getting, in writing.

In Western Australia, "engineer", "structural engineer" and "consulting engineer" are not reserved titles. The reserved ones are things like "Registered Building Engineer". Registration of structural building engineers began on 1 July 2024, with a transition period running to 1 July 2027. From that date, structural engineering work must be done by a registered building engineer, and you can check the Building Engineer Register kept by [Building and Energy at DEMIRS](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/building-and-energy). Until then, the word "engineer" on a card tells you little.

## What a building inspection covers (and what it does not)

A standard building inspection is a visual look at accessible areas of a house. The inspector walks the roof space if it can be entered, checks walls, ceilings, floors, windows, wet areas and drainage, and lists visible defects. You get a report that flags what needs attention and, often, what looks like a bigger problem.

That is genuinely useful when you are buying, and it can shape a negotiation.

What it is not designed to do:

- Decide whether an internal wall is load-bearing.
- Design a beam, lintel or footing.
- Work out why a crack is moving, and whether it is still moving.
- Produce drawings or a certificate that a council or building surveyor will accept for permit purposes.

Where a building report says "seek engineering advice", that is the handover point. The inspector is telling you where their scope ends.

## What a structural inspection covers

A structural inspection is narrower and deeper. Perth engineering firms describe it as an assessment of the foundation, walls and roof structure, load-bearing floors and ceilings, the slab and footings, and any signs of cracking, sagging or shifting. The engineer is asking how the building carries its weight and whether something is changing.

That includes the "why". CSIRO's guidance on foundation maintenance points out that symptoms do not simply reflect the original cause, which is why diagnosis is harder than it looks. A stepped crack in brickwork might be a leaking pipe, an overwatered garden bed, a tree, a rusted lintel or ground movement. Each one has a different fix and a different builder.

Whatever comes back should say what was looked at and what was not.

A structural inspection will not fix anything. Engineers assess and design. Registered builders repair and build.

## Three Perth situations and which report fits

These are anonymised, typical scenarios, not real clients.

**A 1970s double-brick home in Duncraig, kitchen wall to go.** The owners want an open-plan kitchen. A building inspection would describe the wall's condition, but that is the wrong report. CSIRO notes that in full-masonry houses the inner leaf of the external walls and at least some internal walls usually carry upper floors, ceilings and roof, so an internal wall may be doing real work. Removing or altering an element that supports another is outside the building permit exemption in the Building Regulations 2012. This needs an engineer to look at the wall and roof structure, design a beam if the wall can come out at all, and prepare what the permit needs. The fee for that design is not published by any Perth firm, so it is quoted after the engineer sees plans and photos. Start with [how to tell if a wall is load-bearing](/blog/how-to-tell-if-a-wall-is-load-bearing/) and the [wall removal engineering page](/wall-removal-engineering/).

**A stepped crack above a window in a Rockingham house.** The crack runs through the mortar in a stair pattern and was 3 mm wide in spring. CSIRO's categories put cracks under 5 mm at "slight" and 5 to 15 mm at "moderate", but width alone is not a diagnosis, and it is not a promise that the wall is fine. The useful question is whether the crack is moving. A building report gives a snapshot. An engineer can suggest monitoring, look at the lintel and nearby drainage, and give a view on cause.

**A Kalamunda home whose back doors started sticking after a wet winter.** Doors that bind and cornices that crack are classic signs of seasonal clay movement, and the Perth Hills are one of the places where reactive clay is documented. But no source assigns a site class to a suburb, so nobody should tell you your soil type from the postcode. The engineer works from the site: drainage, trees, downpipes, plumbing and what the movement is doing. The pattern over the next year matters more than any label.

## Structural inspection vs building inspection: what each one costs

No Perth engineering firm publishes a fee for a crack report, beam design, site-specific patio certificate or extension design, and none publishes an hourly rate. Engineers quote each job. The price depends on the house, the structure and what the council needs.

The Perth dollar figures that do exist belong to building inspections, and they look like this.

| | Building inspection | Structural inspection by an engineer |
|---|---|---|
| Who does it | A building inspector, sometimes a registered builder or an engineer (check the signature) | An independent structural engineer |
| Question it answers | What is the condition of this house? | How is this structure carrying load, and is it moving? |
| Typical output | Condition report to AS 4349.1 listing visible defects | Written findings on walls, roof, floors, slab or footings, and advice on cause |
| Published Perth price | From $440 including GST; about $495 including GST for an average home; $450 plus GST for a pre-purchase report | None published. Quoted per job |
| Good fit for | Buying a home, a general health check | Cracks, sagging, wall removal, extensions, disputes |
| Not a substitute for | Beam or footing design, engineering certificates | A general defects check of the whole house |

Prices are as published by Perth building-inspection firms and were checked on 30 September 2026. Some firms bundle a timber pest inspection, and some do not say whether GST is included.

Engineering ranges on lead-generation pages and national platforms are not Perth firm prices. The only fixed engineering fees we found published come from interstate firms.

> **Interstate / national figures, not Perth prices.** A national quote platform reports about $560 excluding GST for a residential inspection and report, with a $150 hourly rate and $1,900 for beam design for wall removal, across projects Australia-wide. One Adelaide practice publishes $645 including GST for a structural site inspection (visit, photos, short letter) and $1,295 for an inspection and report. One Queensland practice publishes $885 for a focused structural inspection, such as for wall removal or cracking. These vary by state, scope and whether GST is included. Do not use them to guess a Perth quote.

The government charges around a permit are published, and they are worth knowing so a quote does not surprise you. A certified building permit for a Class 1 or Class 10 building costs 0.19% of the estimated value, not less than $121.00. An uncertified permit is 0.32%, also with a $121.00 minimum. Those are permit costs, not engineering fees.

## How to decide which one you need

Work through your situation in this order.

**You are buying and want a general check.** A pre-purchase building inspection is the normal first step. Read the scope, ask who signs it, and keep an eye out for any line that says "seek engineering advice". If it appears, that is your cue to book a structural inspection.

**You are seeing cracks, sagging, sticking doors or a floor that has moved.** Skip straight to an engineer. A building report will spot the symptom. An engineer's job is to look at cause, movement and risk. Remember that home policies often exclude gradual subsidence and ground movement, so check your own policy's exclusions before you assume cover either way.

**You want to alter, add to or build over something.** Wall removal, an extension or second storey and footing work fall outside the permit exemption, because they change structure, floor area or height. The building surveyor will want engineering. That is a design job, not an inspection, and the [structural engineer cost page](/structural-engineer-cost-perth/) explains why it cannot be priced before someone sees the plans.

**You need a certificate for a patio, carport or shed.** Small freestanding structures can be exempt, but a roofed patio is not a pergola under the exemption, and the numbers are tight: a freestanding Class 10a building must not exceed 10 square metres and 2.4 metres high. For a gable patio in Baldivis, say a $12,000 build, the certified permit percentage works out to about $23, so the $121.00 minimum applies. The council will still want the engineering. Note also that "certificate of structural adequacy" is not a statutory WA form. The Building Act's term is a technical certificate, and councils use their own forms, such as the City of Perth's "Certificate of Structural Sufficiency". A structural engineer's certificate, often called a certificate of structural adequacy or structural sufficiency, is what people usually mean.

**You are in a dispute with a builder or insurer.** Get an independent report from someone with no stake in the outcome of the repair. Building and Energy handles building disputes: building service complaints generally within 6 years of completion, and home building work contract complaints, for contracts between $7,500 and $500,000, generally within 3 years. A Notice of Proposed Complaint must be served at least 14 days before lodging. The Building Act and its regulations are on [legislation.wa.gov.au](https://www.legislation.wa.gov.au).

Not sure which category you fall into? That is common, and a good engineer will say plainly if you do not need one yet. [Send us the details](/#contact), or ring (08) 5550 6237 if you prefer.

Whichever route you take, do not treat a structural inspection as a green light for a particular outcome. An engineer can say what they found and what they recommend. They cannot promise a permit will be approved, and a website should not promise it either. Photos of each crack with a ruler in frame, the age of the house and any plans you hold will sharpen a quote.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a pre-purchase building inspection enough before I buy a house in Perth?

Often it is a sensible first step, and for a house with no visible movement it may be all you need. If the report mentions cracking, sagging, sloping floors or "seek engineering advice", book a structural inspection before you commit. The building report tells you something is there. The engineer tells you what it means.

### Can a building inspector tell me whether a wall is load-bearing?

Not reliably, and a general building report is not designed to answer it. In double-brick homes, some internal walls usually carry upper floors, ceilings and roof, so guessing is risky. An engineer looks at the roof structure and what sits above the wall, then advises on whether a beam could replace it. The permit process needs that engineering in any case.

### How much does a structural inspection cost in Perth?

There is no published price. Perth engineering firms quote per job, and the fee depends on the house, the structure and what the council needs. The figures you can find for Perth, from $440 to about $550, are for building inspections. Interstate and national engineering figures exist but are not Perth prices.

### Will a structural inspection help if I am in a dispute with my builder or insurer?

An independent engineer's written findings can give you a clear, dated account of what is wrong and what is likely causing it, which is a better starting point than opinion. It does not guarantee an outcome. Many home policies exclude gradual subsidence and ground movement, so read your policy. Building and Energy handles building disputes and sets time limits, so act early.
